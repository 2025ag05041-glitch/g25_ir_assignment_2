Information technology in India
The information technology (IT) industry in India comprises information technology services and
business process outsourcing.[1] The IT–BPM sector contributes approximately 7.5% to the GDP of
India.[2] According to NASSCOM, India's technology industry revenue is estimated to reach $315 billion
in FY 2026, up 6.1% from a revised $297 billion in FY 2025.[3][4] In FY 2026, export revenue is
estimated at $246 billion and domestic revenue at $69 billion (up from $224.4 billion in exports and
$58.2 billion in domestic revenue in FY 2025).[3][5][6][7] The IT–BPM sector employs an estimated 5.95
million people directly in FY 2026, up from 5.8 million in FY 2025.[4][5][7] IT units registered with state-
run Software Technology Parks of India (STPI) exported software worth ₹10.64 lakh crore in FY 2024–
25,[8] while combined exports from STPI and Special Economic Zones (SEZs) stood at ₹11.59 lakh crore
in 2021–22.[9]

History
The Electronics Committee also known as the "Bhabha Committee" created a 10-year (1966–1975) plan
laying the foundation for India's IT Service Industries.[10] The industry was born in Mumbai in 1967 with
the establishment of Tata Consultancy Services[11] who in 1977 partnered with Burroughs which began
India's export of IT services.[12] The first software export zone, SEEPZ – the precursor to the modern-day
IT park – was established in Mumbai in 1973. More than 80 percent of the country's software exports
were from SEEPZ in the 1980s.[11]

Automation and layoffs
In the last decade, most of the IT companies developed indigenous R&D and innovation capabilities to
develop homegrown IT products.[15] As the IT–BPM sector evolves, many are concerned that artificial
intelligence (AI) will drive significant automation and destroy jobs in the coming years.[16][17]

The rise of AI-powered code generation tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot has sparked discussions
about their potential impact on programming jobs.[18] Such tools can automate some coding tasks, which
may change the skills required for programming roles.[19] According to the tracking website Layoffs.fyi,
more than 500,000 technology workers worldwide were laid off between 2022 and April 2024.[20][21][22]

A 2024 study published in the Harvard Business Review found that demand for freelance work in writing,
coding and image creation fell after the release of ChatGPT and AI image generators, with job postings in
some categories declining by up to 30 percent.[23][24]

A 2018 article published by the Center for the Advanced Study of India (CASI) at the University of
Pennsylvania noted that India handles 65 percent of global IT offshore work and 40 percent of global
business process outsourcing, and cited a World Bank estimate that 69 percent of jobs in India are
threatened by automation.[25] One report indicates that 640,000 low-skilled service jobs in the IT sector
are at risk due to automation, while only 160,000 mid- to high-skilled positions will be created in the IT
and BPO service sectors.[26] Goldman Sachs has predicted that advances in artificial intelligence (AI)
could potentially automate the equivalent of 300 million full-time jobs globally.[27][28]

In 2024, technology companies including Google, Amazon, Meta, and Cisco announced job cuts,
attributed to a mix of AI adoption, economic conditions and cost reduction.[29] In March 2024, Vineet
Nayar, former CEO of HCL, said that AI could leave IT companies needing up to 70 percent fewer
people, as repetitive and data-entry-heavy work such as coding, testing, maintenance and responding to
trouble tickets is taken over by AI.[30][31]

Attrition rate
The Indian IT-BPM industry has historically had high employee attrition rates.[32][33][34][35][36] In recent
years, the industry has seen a surge in resignations at all levels.[32][34][36]

Major IT hubs

Bengaluru
Bengaluru is a global technology hub and is India's biggest
tech hub.[51] As of fiscal 2016–17, Bengaluru accounted for
38% of total IT exports from India worth $45 billion,
employing 10 lakh people directly and 30 lakh indirectly.[52]

The city is known as the "Silicon Valley of India".[53][54]

Bengaluru is also known as the "startup capital of India"; the
city is home to 44 percent of all Indian unicorn startup
companies as of 2020.[55]

Offices of Oracle and others in
Hyderabad Bengaluru, India

Hyderabad – known for the HITEC City or Cyberabad – is
India's second largest information technology exporter and a
major global IT hub, and the largest bioinformatics hub in
India.[56][57] Hyderabad has emerged as the second largest
city in the country for software exports pipping competitors
Chennai and Pune.[58][59][60]

Pune Amazon Hyderabad campus
Pune is a major centre for IT services and software
development. Companies with large operations in the city
include Infosys, TCS, Accenture, Amdocs, Cognizant, IBM, Capgemini and Tech Mahindra. Several
global capability centres operate in Pune, including those of financial firms such as UBS, Barclays and
BNY. Technology companies founded in the city include Druva, FirstCry and PubMatic.

Chennai



As of 2018, Chennai was India's third-largest exporter of
information technology (IT) and business process outsourcing
(BPO) services, after Bangalore and Hyderabad.[61][62]

TIDEL Park in Chennai was billed as Asia's largest IT park
when it was built.[63][64][62]

Kolkata
The Kolkata Metropolitan Area is the largest IT hub in East
India. Most of the IT parks and offices are located at New Eon IT Park, Pune
Town and Bidhannagar. Salt Lake Electronics Complex in
Salt Lake Sector-V is India's first fully integrated Electronics
Complex.[65] As of 2020, the city's IT sector employed more
than 200,000 people directly. Total export from IT sector was
estimated at ₹25,918 crore in 2021–22.[66] In 2022, Kolkata
generated 20,000 direct jobs in just 6 months, which is an all-
time high for IT industry in East India.[67]

Controversies
Zoho headquarters in Chennai

Recruitment fraud
Some job seekers have used forged experience certificates to
gain entry into the Indian IT
industry.[68][69][70][71][72][73][74][75] These fake documents are
provided by consultancies that are mainly operating out of
Chennai, Hyderabad and Noida.[68][69][70] IT professionals
frequently use proxy interviews to clear interviews, but the
majority of the phoney candidates are rejected during the
interview round.[76]

In response, IT companies have tightened background checks Sector V Salt Lake - the IT hub of Kolkata
on candidates, including through third-party verification
firms.[77]

Tech support scams
A 2017 study of technical support scams published at the
NDSS Symposium found that, of the tech support scams in
which the IPs involved could be geolocated, 85% could be
traced to locations in India.[78] Fraudulent call centres in
India have targeted victims in the United States and
Europe.[79][80][81][82][83][84] Kolkata, Bangalore, Hyderabad, DLF IT Park, New Town
and Mumbai are the main operating locations for these fraud
call centres.[85][86][87][88]



Law enforcement agencies in India, including the Central Bureau of Investigation, have raided such call
centres, in some cases working with the FBI and other foreign agencies.[89]
